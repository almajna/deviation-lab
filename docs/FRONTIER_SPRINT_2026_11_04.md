## Decision

**PIVOT for the requested frontier-discovery sprint. No new experiment was run.**
The strongest new lead is an inspectable provider-metadata trust boundary in
LiteLLM. It is a candidate engineering finding, not a verified exploit, a measured
profit opportunity, or an established original scientific result. The existing
DEVIATION experiments remain valid within their stated synthetic, finite-game
scope, including their negative findings.

No candidate met all six Gate 1 requirements at the evidence level needed for the
proposed claim. Gates 2 and 3 were not entered: there is no newly preregistered
experiment, no new model inference, and no substituted scripted-agent result.
This is a bounded feasibility decision, not a statistical null result or proof
that useful research in this area is impossible. A separate reviewer is to
challenge this committed decision and its source/numerical support.

The literature specialist's conditional GO recommendation for rank 1 is retained,
not presented as unanimous PIVOT. The lead declines to promote that *engineering*
opportunity into the requested scientific experiment: a manipulated denominator is
simple, and the inspected sources do not yet connect it to sustained net allocation
gain under real safeguards or independently measured harm. Absence of a completed
reproduction is not by itself a reason to reject a proposed experiment. Likewise,
observed provider marginal costs are not necessary for every useful routing study;
they are necessary for an empirical **net-profit** claim unless a clearly stated
model or bound replaces them. A narrower latency-integrity experiment could proceed
as engineering work, but that would not establish the stronger proposed result.

The most promising next scientific question is rank 1, scoped to provider telemetry
and allocation integrity. **No question was selected for execution this sprint.**

## What would change the decision

1. Establish the narrow provider-telemetry novelty claim against complete latest
   papers and real implementations, including defenses, not only keyword absence.
2. Execute pinned routing code offline with realistic independently measured
   workload traces, enrolled-provider control and all quota/history safeguards.
   Test default shuffle, streaming TTFT, honest faster providers and independently
   checked token counts. Trace allocation gain rather than only score changes.
3. Define economic scope honestly: measured client cost/latency/quality harm, or
   explicit provider profit bounds with justified cost assumptions. Do not convert
   virtual credits or API prices into observed profits.
4. Produce a general result or broad consequence beyond the trivial denominator
   observation, compare strong published routing/defense baselines and simple
   heuristics, then freeze the threat model, metrics, analysis and stopping rule
   before new outcomes. Existing DEVIATION certificates need a validated scope
   bridge; they cannot simply be relabeled for a different mechanism.
5. Have a fresh reviewer recompute raw actions, source fidelity, allocation and
   economic arithmetic, challenge alternative explanations, and preserve failures.
   Independent human/domain review would still be needed for publication.

This sprint produces a source-grounded decision and an audit of prior evidence.
It does **not** produce a new theorem, a verified new exploit or a frontier result.
No paid spending, live-system exploitation, frontend work, generic framework,
automatic merge, historical-result rewrite or final-holdout evaluation occurred.
