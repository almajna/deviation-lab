## Pre-specified hypothesis and comparison

On the 100 clean-condition tasks, could adaptively choosing which four of eight candidate agents to sample a second time improve the quality of the resulting historical per-task training label under a budget of 12 total agent runs, compared with an equal-information uniform-random assignment of those four extra observations? This is a reallocation of **offline feedback acquisition**, not a method for choosing models for an unseen new task and not an observation of live savings. Hypothesis: adaptive identification yields higher mean held-back empirical utility. Prior work DARS (arXiv:2606.06924) uses repeated observations; SaveRouter (arXiv:2609.37402) uses adaptive feedback allocation. The experimental adaptive rule below is a simple existing sequential-selection idea, not a novel general method or a reproduced SaveRouter algorithm.

Fixed `clean` only because adversarial and noisy conditions represent different interventions, not exchangeable replications. Report all other conditions as excluded, not as negative cases. Each agent/task clean cell has three released repeats; use the third held-back repeat as fixed reference after taking at most two for sampling. If missing, duplicated, extra, negative/nonfinite cost, invalid scores, inconsistent agent identifiers, or zero development cost scale, fail closed; do not cherry-pick complete tasks. The paper's `completion` can be a continuous judge score: require finite [0,1], do not assume a binary ground truth. Costs are source-recorded API accounting, not measured end-to-end deployment cost; equal API-call budgets are not equal dollar budgets.

## Deterministic splits and setup

Hash the 100 task IDs by SHA-256 of `AOB-QUERY-V1|<task_id>`, sorting bytes then ID. First 50 tasks form development; last 50 evaluation. Define development cost range by all 8 × 3 clean observations in the 50 development tasks only. For one evaluation task, sort its three recorded `run_number`s for each agent by SHA-256 of `AOB-REPEAT-V1|<task_id>|<agent_id>|<run_number>`, tie breaking by original run number. The first two are acquisition observations, the last one is held back for evaluation. All policy decisions see only the outcomes of already sampled acquisition observations for that task and the frozen development cost range. No test/heldback outcome and no unsampled acquisition outcome may enter selection or ordering.

The score of an observed agent history `H` is mean of `completion - 0.05 * (cost - development_min) / (development_max - development_min)` over its acquired observations; no clipping. Policy selection maximizes this score; ties broken ascending agent identifier. This weighting is a fixed sensitivity convention, not measured user utility, and test values may exceed the development range. `run_number`s are a replay ordering only, not evidence of temporal deployment decisions; dependence among repeats and calibration of the provided automated/LLM judge remain unverified.

## Two fixed policies

Both see the first acquisition observation for every agent (8 calls). For **adaptive**, sample a second observation for the four agents with the greatest score after this initial round (4 calls), then select the highest final observed mean across all eight agents. For **uniform-random**, assign the four second observations to the four agents ordered smallest by SHA-256 of `AOB-UNIFORM-V1|<task_id>|<agent_id>` (4 calls), then select the highest final observed mean across all eight. Both have the same information set and 12 calls; neither knows unsampled outcomes. Development costs determine a shared scale. Tie counts and selected-agent histograms are diagnostic, not selection criteria.
