# Theorem 3: outside-tie qualification

This is a separate, exploratory analytic check of [arXiv:2601.21839v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21839v3), not a tie-free counterexample and not part of the frozen 2,520-game test. It depends on using the perfect-rationality table's strict-indicator convention for ties with abstention. At such a tie the indicators do not sum to one, so this is at least an under-specified normalization/tie-breaking issue; other tie rules can change the equilibrium.

Let Θ=(low, high), v0=0. For provider 1, q=(2,3), p=(1,3), c=(1/2,1); for provider 2, q=(1/5,2/5), p=(6/5,12/5), c=(7/10,7/5). Values are (1,0), (-1,-2) and margins (1/2,2), (1/2,1). Quality weakly increases, cost, price and margin strictly increase, and values of distinct *providers* never tie. At (high,low), provider 1 ties v0 exactly.

Set t=e^β. Under the paper's softmax allocation at that profile, provider 1's utility is 2t/(2t+1). If it switches to low its utility is t²/[2(t²+t+1)]; the former is strictly larger for all t>0 because cross-multiplication reduces to t(2t²+3t+4)>0. Provider 2's utility is 1/[2(2t+1)]. If it switches to high its utility is 1/(2t²+1); the first is larger whenever 2t²-4t-1>0, in particular for all t≥3. Thus the profile is a finite-β Nash equilibrium for every β≥ln 3.

Under the stated strict-winner indicator at β=∞, the selected provider ties the outside option and has utility zero; changing to low wins and gives 1/2. The profile therefore is not a Nash equilibrium under that literal indicator. Under a rule awarding this tie to provider 1 instead, the conclusion changes. Moreover, finite-β shares at the tied profile converge to 1/2, rather than the strict-indicator value of zero. Theorem 3's proof invokes convergence and a strictly positive cross-provider value gap, but needs a non-tie assumption involving v0 or an appropriately matched limit tie rule as well. We make no claim about the theorem under the added no-outside-ties assumption.
